## The symptom pattern

The chain brake is meant to be an emergency stop: pull it forward — by hand,
or automatically on kickback — and the chain should stop dead. The fault here
is that it doesn't. The lever moves, it engages, and the chain keeps
spinning anyway, or slows without actually stopping.

This is worth taking seriously precisely because it's rarely obvious in
normal cutting. The brake only gets tested in an emergency, so a saw can run
for a long time with a brake that no longer works before anyone finds out —
at exactly the moment it matters most.

## What's actually happening

Makita's own manual for this saw lists this exact symptom in its
troubleshooting table, in the same terms owners report it: "chain does not
stop even the chain brake is engaged." Their stated cause is a worn brake
band — the band inside the clutch drum that the brake mechanism clamps down
to arrest the chain. As it wears, it loses the friction needed to stop the
drum quickly, and eventually stops working at all even though the lever
still moves and clicks normally.

It's worth separating this from two things it can look like. A chain that
won't run at all, brake engaged or not, usually just means the brake is
still on — release it first. And a saw that vibrates abnormally is something
Makita trace to a loose or mistensioned guide bar and chain, which is a
completely different fault from a worn brake band and doesn't affect braking
at all.

## Working through it

Unplug the saw before touching anything — this is not a fault to
investigate live, and there's no diagnostic value in running a saw with a
suspect brake to see what happens.

With the saw unplugged, work the brake lever by hand and confirm it travels
its full range and engages with a positive click. If the lever itself is
stiff, obstructed, or doesn't latch, that's a mechanical problem with the
lever or linkage rather than the band, though the manual doesn't split that
case out separately.

If the lever engages properly and the chain still isn't held, Makita's
troubleshooting table gives one answer: the brake band is worn. There's no
inspection step offered for checking band wear directly, because Makita
don't intend for an owner to open the clutch drum on this saw.

## The fix

Makita's instruction is unambiguous, and it's worth quoting rather than
softening: stop using the tool immediately, and ask your local authorised
service centre. That's their standard remedy for a worn brake band on this
model, alongside a cable fault and general tool malfunction elsewhere in the
same table. There's no owner-level repair path offered, and given what a
chain brake is for, that's not a corner worth cutting yourself.

## Verdict

Get it looked at rather than get it back into use. A chain saw is a
comparatively inexpensive tool in this class, and a service-centre brake
repair is worth pricing against a replacement saw before deciding which way
to go — but that's a decision to make with the saw sitting unused, not one to
make by continuing to cut with a brake that doesn't reliably stop the chain.
