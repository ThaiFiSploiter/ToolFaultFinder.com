## The symptom pattern

The primer's been pressed, the choke's set, and the starter cord has been
pulled again and again — nothing. On a petrol brushcutter that reads like a
dead engine, but Einhell's own troubleshooting table for the GE-BC 43 AS
lists only three possible causes for "the equipment does not start," and
two of them are things anyone can check in the garden with no tools beyond
the multifunction key supplied with the machine.

## What Einhell's own table says

Section 12 of the manual gives these causes for the equipment not
starting, in the order Einhell lists them: the correct starting procedure
wasn't followed, a sooted or damp spark plug, or an incorrect carburettor
setting. The first two have free, immediate remedies in the manual's own
words — "follow the instructions for starting" and "clean the spark plug
or replace it with a new one." The third is routed straight to a service
centre: Einhell's carburettor-settings section states plainly that
adjustments "may only be made by authorized customer service personnel."

The starting procedure itself is easy to get slightly wrong. Einhell's
cold-start sequence is a specific order — prime the carburettor by
pressing the primer bulb 10 times, set the choke, actuate the
decompression lever, then pull the starter cord in short sharp tugs rather
than one long pull — and a warm engine (idle for under 15–20 minutes) uses
a shorter sequence with the choke left off. Running the wrong sequence, or
the right sequence out of order, is enough on its own to make a perfectly
sound engine refuse to start.

The spark plug is the second most common cause, and Einhell gives an exact
spec for checking it: a sparking gap of 0.6mm, refitted to a torque of
12–15 Nm, cleaned with a copper wire brush if it's dirty rather than
replaced outright unless it's actually damaged. The manual's own
instruction before touching it is to pull the spark boot plug off first.

## Working through it

Start with the procedure, not the hardware: run through the correct
cold-start or warm-start sequence exactly as Einhell lays it out, since a
missed step is the cheapest explanation and needs nothing removed. If that
genuinely doesn't fix it, pull the spark boot plug, remove the spark plug
with the supplied tool, and check it's clean, dry and gapped to 0.6mm —
clean it with a wire brush first and only fit a new Champion RCJ7Y (or
equivalent) if it's actually sooted, damp or damaged. Refit it to the
stated torque before trying to start the engine again. If a confirmed-good
plug and the correct start sequence still don't get it running, that's the
carburettor, and Einhell's own manual says not to adjust it yourself.

## Verdict

This is a cheap fault to diagnose because Einhell's own list is short and
two of the three causes are free. Most "won't start" reports trace to the
starting sequence or the spark plug rather than anything mechanically
wrong with the engine. If both check out clean and it still won't fire,
the carburettor is the manufacturer's own answer — book it in rather than
start adjusting screws on the strength of a guess, since Einhell doesn't
publish a user-level setting procedure for it.
