## The symptom pattern

A blade snaps — sometimes an old one, sometimes a brand new one within
minutes of fitting it — and it's tempting to assume a bad batch or a faulty
machine. Axminster's own manual for the AP2920B treats blade breakage as a
setup problem with three named causes, and none of them is the blade
itself.

## What's actually happening

Bandsaw blades run under tension around two wheels and are guided through
the cut by bearings on either side. Get any of that wrong and the blade is
working against itself: too little tension and it flexes and fatigues where
it shouldn't; guides set too tight and it's pinched every time it moves;
feed the timber too fast and the blade is asked to remove more material
than it's rated for. The manual states plainly that too little blade
tension can cause breakage — it's the first thing worth checking, and the
cheapest.

## Working through it

Start with tension. The manual's spec is concrete: with the tensioning
lever set to full tension, the blade should give roughly 1cm when pressed
at its left-hand side, and the gauge on the front of the upper wheel should
read tension on. Blades stretch slightly with use, so a blade tensioned
correctly when it went on can still slacken off over time — worth
rechecking periodically, not just after fitting a new one.

If tension is correct, move to the guides. The manual gives real figures
here too: the upper and lower guide bearings sit around 2mm behind the
blade's gullet, with no more than 0.5mm clearance to the blade itself, and
the thrust bearings behind them sit around 1mm off the blade, touching it
only once it's under cutting pressure. A thrust bearing left too tight
rides against the blade the whole time it's running rather than just during
a cut, which is exactly the kind of steady extra load that ends in a
snapped blade.

Last, look at how the saw is being fed. Pushing timber through faster than
the blade and motor combination can manage is the manual's third named
cause, and it's the easiest one to fix without tools — just slow down.

## Verdict

Blade breakage on the AP2920B is a setup problem the manufacturer names in
its own words, and every cause it gives is free to correct: retension,
reset the guides to spec, ease off the feed rate. Buy a replacement blade
for the one that's already gone, but there's no reason to spend on anything
else, and no reason to suspect the machine itself is at fault.
