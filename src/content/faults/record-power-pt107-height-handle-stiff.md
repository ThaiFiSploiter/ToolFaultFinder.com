## The symptom pattern

Everything about the machine still cuts and planes normally — the fault
is purely in winding the thicknessing height hand wheel up or down. It
turns with noticeably more resistance than it used to, or binds in
places rather than moving smoothly through its range, while the motor
and cutters show no sign of a problem at all.

## What Record Power's manual says

The troubleshooting table gives this exactly one named cause: "Height
handles are hard to turn: 1. Dust has collected on the mechanisms
inside the base — 1. Clean and lubricate the mechanisms inside the
base." The maintenance section backs this up with the specific part
that collects it: "Clean the thicknessing bed spindles on a regular
basis to prevent the build-up of wood chips and dust, lubricate with
silicone spray."

The manual is also specific about what not to reach for: "Do not use
ordinary oil as this will attract dust." A squirt of household oil might
free the wheel up in the short term, but it turns the spindle into a
dust magnet and makes the problem worse the next time the machine runs.

## Working through it

Isolate the machine from the power supply first — this is maintenance
work right at the base of the thicknesser, not a job to do live. Clean
the drive gear of dust with a brush or compressed air (wear eye
protection if you're blowing it out), then focus on the thicknessing
bed spindles themselves, where the manual says the build-up actually
collects. Wipe them down and apply a light coat of silicone spray —
Record Power names their own CWA195 specifically — rather than any
general-purpose oil. Work the hand wheel through its full travel a few
times afterwards to confirm it's moving freely again before putting the
machine back to work.

## Verdict

Free beyond the cost of a can of silicone spray, and entirely an owner
job. This is exactly the kind of fault that's cheaper to prevent than
cure — a planer thicknesser throws off a lot of fine dust, and cleaning
and lubricating the height mechanism on the same schedule as the rest
of the machine's maintenance keeps it from binding in the first place.
