## The symptom pattern

The saw is running, but it's visibly smoking more than it should — and
Qualcast's own manual for the PC40 is worth reading carefully here, because
it covers two completely different faults that both show up as "smoke" and
doesn't treat them as the same thing. One is in the engine troubleshooting
table proper, under "Smokes Excessively." The other is a warning tucked
into the maintenance section about the bar and chain, which never uses the
word "troubleshooting" at all but describes exactly the same visible
symptom from an entirely different cause.

## What's actually happening

If the smoke is coming off the guide bar and chain rather than the engine,
the manual's maintenance section is explicit about why: "running the saw
dry or with too little [bar and chain] oil will decrease cutting
efficiency, shorten saw chain life, cause rapid dulling of chain, and lead
to excessive wear of bar from overheating," and states plainly that "too
little oil is evidenced by smoke or bar discoloration" (the manual's own
spelling). That's friction
heat at the bar, not the engine burning anything wrong — the automatic
oiler isn't keeping the moving chain lubricated against the bar.

If the smoke is coming from the engine and muffler area, that's the
troubleshooting table's own "Smokes Excessively" row, and it names two
different causes: an incorrectly mixed fuel (the manual specifies a 40:1
petrol-to-oil ratio for this two-stroke engine), or the carburettor's
mixture adjustment itself being set wrong. The manual draws a clear line
between the two — the fuel mixture is something you fix yourself by
draining and remixing, while the carburettor adjustment is specifically
routed to an Authorized Service Center rather than given as a user
procedure.

## Working through it

Let the saw cool and disconnect the spark plug lead before you check
anything that doesn't need the engine running. The first real step is
working out which kind of smoke you actually have, because the two causes
share nothing in common.

For bar/chain smoke: check the oil tank has chain, bar and sprocket oil in
it, then start the saw briefly and watch the tip of the bar — the manual
says a saw with clear oil passages will throw a visible spray of oil within
seconds of starting. If it doesn't, the automatic oiler isn't doing its job
and the bar is running dry, which is exactly what produces this kind of
smoke and the bar discolouration that goes with it.

For engine smoke: check what fuel is actually in the tank. If you're not
certain it was mixed at 40:1, or it's been sitting a while, drain it and
refill with a freshly mixed batch at the correct ratio — this alone fixes
the manual's first named cause. If the smoke continues on correctly mixed
fresh fuel, the remaining cause is the carburettor's own mixture setting,
and the manual doesn't give a user adjustment for that; it goes to a
service centre.

## Verdict

Neither of this fault's real causes needs a part, and both are worth ruling
out before anyone touches a carburettor. Bar/chain smoke is an oil-tank and
oiler check; engine smoke is first a fuel-mixture check. Only if correctly
mixed fresh fuel doesn't stop the smoke does this become a carburettor
adjustment job, and the manual is specific that this one goes to an
Authorized Service Center rather than being something to adjust at home.
