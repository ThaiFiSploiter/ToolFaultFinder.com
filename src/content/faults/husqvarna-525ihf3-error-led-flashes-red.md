## The symptom pattern

The 525iHF3's handle carries its own Error LED, and it can do one of two
things when something's wrong: flash red, or stay lit solid red. Husqvarna's
own troubleshooting table treats these as two different conditions with
two different outcomes, not degrees of the same problem — so the first
thing worth checking is which one you're actually looking at.

## What's actually happening

A flashing red Error LED covers three separate causes in Husqvarna's
table: the cutting attachment has jammed under an overload, the product
has overheated, or you're pressing the power trigger and the activate
button at the same time (a built-in interlock, not a fault). None of these
three implies the others — they're genuinely different things the LED can
be reporting, and the manual gives each one its own fix.

A solid red Error LED is where the table changes tone entirely: there's no
clear-this-yourself step given at all, only an instruction to contact a
servicing agent.

## Working through it

Start by watching the LED for a few seconds to confirm whether it's
flashing or staying lit — the rest of this depends on which.

If it's flashing and the cutting attachment feels jammed or bogged down:
press and hold the On/Off button to switch the product off, remove the
battery, and clear whatever's caught in the cutting attachment before you
try again.

If it's flashing but nothing feels jammed, the next cause in Husqvarna's
table is simple overheating — let the product cool down before you try to
restart it.

If neither of those fits, check your own grip: pressing the power trigger
and the activate button at the same time is the third cause the manual
names, and it isn't a fault in the product at all. Release both, and
Husqvarna's manual states the product becomes active again immediately.

If the Error LED is lit solid red rather than flashing, stop trying to
clear it yourself. The manual gives no further diagnostic step for this
state beyond contacting your servicing agent.

## Verdict

A flashing red Error LED is almost always something you can clear in a
couple of minutes — a jam, overheating, or simply releasing a control
you're pressing by accident — and none of it needs a part or a workshop
visit. A solid red Error LED is the one exception: Husqvarna's own manual
treats that as beyond user troubleshooting, so that's the point to book it
in rather than keep trying fixes the manual never offered in the first
place.
