## The symptom pattern

Two different complaints show up on this machine, and Einhell's own
manual treats them as separate faults with separate causes: the
suction/blow selector switch becomes stiff and won't flip cleanly
between modes, or the motor doesn't start at all when the switch is
pressed. They're easy to conflate because both make the tool feel like
it's "not working properly" — but the manual gives each one a different
diagnosis.

## What Einhell's own documentation says

The switch problem is covered in the manual's cleaning section, not the
fault table: "Dirt and grime can make the switch (blower/vacuum) more
difficult to operate as a result of the material that swirls up off the
ground. When this occurs, simply flip the switch back and forth a few
times, which should bring it back to the normal feel." No tools, no
disassembly — just working the lever itself.

A motor that won't start at all is covered separately, in Section 11's
troubleshooting table, under "Motor does not start": no voltage present
(check the cord and fuses), a defective power cord, or disconnected
motor terminals — and the last two both carry the same remedy, "have the
unit checked by a customer service workshop." Einhell doesn't publish a
continuity test or any owner-level step for either. Section 8.2 reiterates
the same limit: "Should the tool experience problems beyond those
mentioned above, let only an authorized professional or a customer
service shop perform an inspection."

## Working through it

Check the cord for visible damage and the fuse first if the motor's
silent — it's free and it's the one thing the manual actually hands the
owner. If that's fine and the switch itself feels gritty or won't flip
properly, work it back and forth a few times before assuming anything's
broken; that alone clears most of these. Don't go further than that on
your own: the manual is explicit that a damaged power cable "must be
replaced by the manufacturer or its after-sales service or similarly
trained personnel to avoid danger," and a motor that still won't start
after the cord and fuse check out is the same territory.

## Verdict

The switch fault is as easy as this site sees — a genuine no-cost fix
with a one-line procedure straight from the manufacturer. A dead motor
is a different question. Einhell doesn't give a DIY path past the cord
and fuse, which means the real remedy is a paid workshop inspection, and
on a leaf vac in this price bracket that quote is worth getting before
deciding whether to repair or replace — there's a real chance it costs
close to a new machine.
