## The symptom pattern

The starter cord pulls and the engine either doesn't fire at all, or
catches briefly and dies straight away on a cold start. Husqvarna's own
manual for the T525 gives this its own troubleshooting table —
"The engine does not start" — and, unusually for a chainsaw fault, spreads
the likely cause across four unrelated systems rather than pointing at one.

## What's actually happening

Husqvarna's table checks four things, each with its own fix: the starter
pawls (the mechanism the pull-cord engages to turn the engine over),
what's actually in the fuel tank, the spark plug's condition and gap, and
whether the engine has flooded from being pulled over repeatedly with the
choke out. None of these four causes implies the others — a blocked
starter pawl doesn't point to a fuel problem, and a flooded engine isn't
evidence of a bad spark plug. The table treats them as genuinely separate
things to rule out in turn.

## Working through it

Start with the starter pawls, since a blocked pawl stops the engine
turning over at all regardless of fuel or spark. Husqvarna's fix is to
adjust or replace them and clean around them; if that doesn't free them,
the manual stops giving a further home step and routes it to an approved
service workshop.

Next, check what's in the fuel tank. The wrong fuel type means draining
the tank and refilling correctly. If chain oil has ended up in the fuel
tank instead of fuel, the manual's advice depends on what you've already
done: if you haven't tried starting it yet, just drain the tank — but if
you've already attempted to start the saw on chain oil, Husqvarna says to
speak to your servicing dealer rather than keep pulling the cord.

With the engine stopped and the spark plug cap removed, check the plug
itself is dry and clean — a wet or fouled plug won't fire reliably. Then
check the electrode gap against the model's own spec: 0.75mm, on the
recommended NGK CMR6A plug. Clean or correct the gap if it's off.

If none of that explains it and you've been repeatedly pulling the
starter with the choke fully out, the engine may simply be flooded. Remove
and clean the spark plug, lay the saw on its side with the spark plug hole
facing away from you, and pull the starter rope handle 6-8 times to clear
the excess fuel before refitting the plug and trying again — Husqvarna
gives this exact procedure rather than a general "try again later."

## Verdict

Nothing in Husqvarna's own troubleshooting table for this fault requires
a workshop visit, except a starter pawl that won't free up after cleaning
and adjustment. Fuel and spark plug checks cost nothing but a few minutes
and catch most cases; the flooded-engine clearing procedure is worth
trying on its own before assuming the saw needs parts, since it's a direct
consequence of how it was started rather than a fault with it.
