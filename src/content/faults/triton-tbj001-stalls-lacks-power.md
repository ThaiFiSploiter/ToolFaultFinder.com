## The symptom pattern

The jointer starts a cut normally, then the motor bogs down or loses power
partway through, instead of running cleanly to the end of the slot. It
doesn't always point to the same thing — Triton's own manual lists seven
separate possible causes under exactly this symptom, which is a sign the
fault genuinely can come from several different places rather than one
obvious culprit.

## Working through it in order

Start with the Dust Port. Triton's troubleshooting table names a clogged
Dust Port as a cause of the tool stalling, and the fix is as simple as it
sounds — chippings backing up into the cutting area load the motor the
same way a blocked vacuum nozzle chokes suction. Clear it before looking
at anything else.

Next, check the blade itself for dullness or visible damage. The manual
lists a dull or damaged blade as a cause of stalling, and separately lists
the same cause for a blade that's burning the workpiece rather than
cutting it — so scorch marks on the cut edge are a second, independent
sign pointing at the same fix: replace the blade.

If the blade is fine, move to the motor brushes. The manual lists worn
brushes as a stalling cause, and its Maintenance section is specific about
what to look for: "excessively worn brushes may cause loss of power,
intermittent failure, or visible sparking." Reaching them means removing
the two Handle Screws and lifting the main handle housing away from the
motor, exactly as the manual's own numbered brush-replacement procedure
describes — and if they're worn, that procedure also tells you how to fit
the new pair.

If you're running the jointer through an extension cord, check its gauge
and length next — a cord that's too thin or too long for the load is
listed as its own cause, with the fix simply being a shorter cord or a
heavier gauge.

Motor overheating is next on the list: switch off, unplug, and let the
tool cool to room temperature, then inspect and clean the ventilation
slots before trying again.

If you've worked through all of the above and the jointer still stalls,
the one cause left on Triton's list is a fault in the motor or its
bearings — and that's the single cause on the whole table that Triton
doesn't give a home fix for. Their own stated solution is to have it
serviced by an authorised Triton service centre.

## Verdict

Most of what's on this list costs nothing or close to it. Clearing the
dust port, checking the cord and letting the tool cool are all free, and
a dull blade or worn brushes are both cheap parts the manual walks you
through fitting yourself — brush replacement included as a proper numbered
procedure, not brushed off as a service job. It's only the last item on
Triton's own list, a fault in the motor or bearings, that gets routed to a
service centre rather than a home fix, and at that point it's worth
weighing the repair against the cost of a new 760W jointer before
committing to it.
