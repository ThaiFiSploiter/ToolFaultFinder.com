## The symptom pattern

The grinder was running, the power dropped out for a moment — an RCD trip, a
loose extension lead, someone else unplugging the socket — and now it won't
start again. The switch is still pressed and locked forward. Nothing
happens when the power comes back. It looks exactly like the kind of fault
that means a trip to a repair shop, and Einhell's own manual explains why it
almost certainly isn't.

## What Einhell's own manual says

The TC-AG 115/750 has a safety feature called a restart safeguard, and the
manual states its behaviour plainly: "If the mains power supply is
interrupted when the switch is locked in the On position, the electric tool
will not run after the power supply is restored. For the equipment to run,
the switch must be turned off and then on again." That's the entire
mechanism — the grinder is refusing to spring back to life on its own after
any power loss while locked on, specifically so it can't restart
unexpectedly while unattended.

The switch itself is a two-stage action, and the manual spells out both
directions: "To switch on, press down the On/Off switch (2) at the back,
push it forwards and then press it down at the front. To switch off the
angle grinder, depress the On/Off switch (2) at the back. The On/Off switch
(2) will jump back into its starting position." Skipping any one of those
three motions on restart won't start the tool and can easily be mistaken
for the grinder itself being dead.

Two other things the manual names explicitly are not owner repairs. A
damaged power cable "must be replaced by the manufacturer or its
after-sales service or similarly trained personnel." And a motor that's
sparking heavily during use points at worn carbon brushes, which the manual
says should only be checked and replaced "by a qualified electrician" —
unlike some grinders where a brush swap is a straightforward DIY job, this
one is explicit that it isn't.

## Working through it

Start by fully switching the grinder off — depress the back of the switch
and let it spring back to its rest position — then go through the complete
start sequence again: back, forward, front. That resolves the restart
safeguard condition in seconds and costs nothing. If it still won't run at
all after that, check the lead for obvious damage without opening anything
up; a damaged cable needs replacing by a qualified person, not a DIY splice.
If the grinder runs but throws noticeably more sparks from the motor
housing than it used to, that's the brushes, and Einhell's manual is clear
that's also not a parts-and-screwdriver job on this model.

## Verdict

Nearly always a free, immediate fix and not really a fault: the restart
safeguard doing its job after a power interruption, cleared by switching
fully off and restarting properly. The two things that are genuine faults —
a damaged cable or worn carbon brushes — both get routed by Einhell's own
manual to a qualified electrician or authorised service rather than an
owner-level repair, so there's nothing to buy or attempt at home for either
of those; the grinder itself isn't written off, just handed to someone
qualified to open the motor housing.
