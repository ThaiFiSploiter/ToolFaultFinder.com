## The symptom pattern

The jigsaw bogs down mid-cut, cuts in fits and starts rather than smoothly,
or — easiest to confirm — you can actually see sparking through the motor
vents when it's running. Triton's own manual for the TJS001 names this
exact combination as the signature of worn carbon brushes: "loss of power,
intermittent failure, or visible sparking." All three point the same way,
so any one of them is reason enough to check.

## Replacing the brushes

This is a home job, not a workshop one — Triton's manual gives it as a full
numbered procedure. With the tool unplugged, remove the Brush Access Screw
and pull off the cover at the rear of the tool. A smaller retaining screw
lets you lift the brush frame clear by about 12mm; disconnect the
electrical connector before sliding the old, worn brush out through the
frame.

Fitting the new one is the same process in reverse: feed the new brush's
wire and connector through the frame first, seat the brush, then reconnect
the electrical connector. Secure the frame back into its recess with its
screw and check by eye that it's sitting correctly — then do the exact same
thing on the second brush. Triton's manual is specific on this point: both
brushes are always replaced together, never just the one that looks worse.

Refit the Base Cover and the Brush Access Screw, keeping the mains cable
clear and the Variable Speed Dial lined up properly, then run the jigsaw
with no blade fitted — slow first, then faster — to confirm it's running
cleanly before putting a blade back in and using it.

## Verdict

Triton doesn't deflect this one to a service centre as the only option; the
manual lists an authorised Triton service centre as an *alternative*, not
the default. A pair of carbon brushes costs very little set against a
replacement 750W jigsaw, so once the sparking, power loss or intermittent
cutting has confirmed it's the brushes, this is a straightforward and cheap
fix to do yourself.
