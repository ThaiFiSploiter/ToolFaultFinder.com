## The symptom pattern

The washer switches on, the motor clearly runs, but when you squeeze the
trigger lever almost nothing comes out — or the jet surges and dies instead
of holding steady. It's an unsettling fault because the motor sounds fine,
which makes it easy to assume the pump itself has failed. Einhell's own
fault table says otherwise: every cause it lists for this is on the water
side, not inside the pump.

## What Einhell's own table says

Section 12's "Possible causes of failure" table gives a short, specific
list for "the motor starts but there is no pressure build-up": the water
intake filter is clogged, there's an air intake somewhere in the water
inlet, the discharge nozzle is too large, or the water cock itself is
closed. A separate row for "the operating pressure is irregular" gives one
cause — again, air drawn in through the water inlet — with the same
remedy as above: check the supply hose and its connectors, and replace
them if they're not sealing properly.

The intake filter check has its own short procedure elsewhere in the
manual (Section 8.2.1): unscrew the coupling for the water inlet hose from
the machine and clean the exposed inlet sieve under running water. Einhell
is explicit about why this matters — a clogged filter "could jeopardize
operation of the pump" if left unchecked, which is as close as the manual
comes to saying the fault can get worse if ignored.

## Working through it

Start with the tap, since it's the fastest thing to rule out — the fault
table treats a closed water cock as a direct cause, not just something to
glance at. Then clean the intake filter using the Section 8.2.1 procedure,
since that's the first cause listed for the exact symptom of a running
motor with no pressure. From there, check the supply hose and its fittings
for a loose or poorly-seated connection that could be drawing in air, and
only then look at the nozzle itself for damage or the wrong size.

## Verdict

Every cause Einhell lists for this fault has a straightforward owner-level
fix, and none of them route to a service centre. Work through the tap, the
filter, the hose connections and the nozzle in that order, and most cases
resolve at the first or second step. There's no reason to assume the pump
has failed before ruling out the water side first.
