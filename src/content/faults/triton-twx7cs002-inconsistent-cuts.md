## The symptom pattern

The TWX7CS002 contractor saw module bolts into Triton's TWX7 Workcentre and
reads, on paper, like a fixed-table saw — set the Protractor Gauge or Rip
Fence to a number and the cut should match it. The fault owners report is
that it doesn't: a mitre comes out a degree or two off, a bevel doesn't sit
flush against the gauge's own reading, or a rip cut that starts at the right
width drifts narrower or wider by the far end of the board. Nothing about
the saw looks damaged, which is usually what prompts the search.

## What's actually happening

Triton's own troubleshooting table for this module gives two named causes
for exactly this symptom, in this order. The first is mechanical: the Rip
Fence or Protractor Gauge isn't clamped as firmly as it needs to be, and
flexes slightly under the sideways load a cut puts on it — enough to throw
the result off without the fence visibly moving beforehand. The second is
calibration: the blade itself has drifted out of true against the table
surface, which happens over time from transport, vibration and the repeated
clamping and unclamping the module goes through on a jobsite or in a
workshop. Both are named directly in the manual, not inferred.

## Working through it

Start with the fence, not the blade — it's the simpler check and the one
Triton's table lists first. Push firmly against the Rip Fence and the
Protractor Gauge by hand, loading them the way a cut would. If either gives
even slightly, refasten it properly and run a test cut before assuming
anything deeper is wrong. While you're at the Protractor Gauge, check the
sacrificial wood strip fitted to it — once that's worn or cut through it
stops backing the workpiece where it meets the fence, and the manual names
a worn strip as its own cause of drift.

If the fence holds firm and the fault persists, move to blade calibration.
Raise the blade to full height, lock the height lock, and hold a set square
against the table surface and the blade face. If it's not square, the
bevel angle adjuster brings it true — check it at both 0° and 45°, since the
two ends are trimmed independently. The step people skip is resetting the
0° and 45° trimming screws afterwards: without that, the pointer on the
bevel angle gauge keeps reading the old, wrong position even once the blade
itself has been corrected, so the gauge lies by exactly the amount you just
fixed. Finally, make sure the bevel angle locking lever is fully engaged
before cutting — a lever that isn't locked lets the angle creep mid-cut,
which looks like the same fault but has nothing to do with calibration.

## Verdict

Worth doing every time, because there's nothing to buy — it's a set square,
a few turns of an adjuster, and about fifteen minutes. This isn't a module
that's failed; it's one that's worked loose or drifted the way any
clamp-together saw table will, and Triton's manual treats it as routine
enough to give it a named procedure rather than a trip to a service centre.
