## The symptom pattern

You wind the tailstock up to support a spindle and it creeps back along the
bed as soon as the tool touches the wood, or you go to lock the tailstock
or the tool rest holder in place and it just won't bite — the lever
tightens but the thing still slides. Both are the same underlying
mechanism behaving badly, and Record Power's own manual, which covers both
the Coronet Envoy and the larger Coronet Regent, gives a specific cause
list for each.

## What Record Power's own manual says

For a tailstock that moves once pressure is on it, the manual names three
causes in order: too much force being wound into the tailstock against the
workpiece, the locking lever not actually secured, or dirt on the mating
surfaces between the tailstock and the bed stopping the clamp from
gripping. All three are free checks — adjust how hard you're winding the
handwheel, confirm the lever is tight, and clean the bed and the
tailstock's underside if they're dusty with shavings.

If the tailstock — or the tool rest holder, which shares the same clamp
design — won't lock in place at all, Record Power gives a single cause:
the clamp itself is set incorrectly, and a specific adjustment fixes it.
The manual's maintenance chapter explains the mechanism: underneath the
tool rest holder or tailstock is a bolt with a nylon locking nut that sets
how hard the clamp bites the bed. Turning that nut clockwise with a 16mm
wrench increases the clamping action; turning it anti-clockwise reduces it.
The manual also notes that stiffness in the clamp can separately be caused
by timber residue or other debris on the bed, which is worth ruling out
before touching the nut at all.

## Working through it

For a tailstock that creeps under load, start with technique rather than
the machine: use only as much force as it takes to hold the workpiece
between centres, not the full travel of the handwheel. Then check the
locking lever is actually engaged, and clean the bed and tailstock contact
surfaces if there's any sawdust or shavings built up. If the tailstock or
tool rest holder won't lock at all regardless of the lever, that's the
clamp adjustment: slide the item off the bed, find the nylon locking nut on
its underside, and adjust it with a 16mm wrench until the clamping action
holds properly when refitted.

## Verdict

Nothing here is a developing fault or a part wearing out — Record Power's
own manual treats both versions of this as a setup and adjustment issue,
with a specific procedure for the one case that needs more than technique.
Work through force, the locking lever and cleanliness first, then the
clamp's nylon locking nut if the holder still won't bite. No tools beyond a
16mm wrench, and nothing to buy.
