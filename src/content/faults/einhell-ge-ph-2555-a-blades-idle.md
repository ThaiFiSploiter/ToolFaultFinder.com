## The symptom pattern

You let go of the throttle lever expecting the cutting blades to settle to a
stop the way they're supposed to between cuts — and they keep moving, maybe
slowly, maybe not much slower than when you were squeezing the trigger. The
engine itself sounds fine. Nothing's smoking or stalling. It's easy to treat
this as background wear on an older machine, but Einhell's own manual names
it directly and gives a specific, user-level fix.

## What Einhell's own manual says

Einhell's manual addresses this exact symptom directly in its carburettor
settings section, without going into what inside the drive train is
responding to idle speed: "If the idling speed is so fast that the cutting
blades do not
reach a standstill, the idling speed must be reduced by turning the idling
speed screw (Fig. 16/Item 5) counterclockwise until the cutting blades
stop."

The same section is equally direct about where the user's role ends: "Settings
on the carburetor may only be made by authorized customer service personnel" —
except for this specific idle-speed screw, which the manual hands straight to
the owner, by name, with a direction to turn it in. It also specifies the idle
speed should only be set once the machine is properly warm, since a cold
engine's idle behaviour isn't representative.

## Working through it

Warm the trimmer up fully before testing anything — judging idle speed cold
will send you adjusting a screw that didn't need it. Once warm, release the
throttle lever completely and watch the blades for a few seconds rather than
glancing away immediately; slow creeping movement counts just as much as
blades still running at speed. If they don't stop, find the idling speed
screw (Fig. 16, item 5 in Einhell's manual) and turn it counterclockwise in
small increments, letting the engine settle and rechecking the blades after
each small turn. Stop as soon as they come to a complete standstill — there's
no benefit to turning it further once that's achieved, and overshooting it
can leave the engine idling too low or stalling.

## Verdict

Worth fixing immediately, and genuinely free to fix: this is a documented,
owner-level carburettor adjustment, not a trip to a service centre. What it
isn't is optional or cosmetic — a hedge trimmer whose blades keep moving at
idle is still an active cutting tool every time you pause between sections of
hedge, which is exactly the kind of moment grip and attention relax. If the
idle screw alone doesn't bring the blades to a full stop, Einhell's manual is
explicit that any further carburettor work needs authorized service rather
than more adjustment at home.
