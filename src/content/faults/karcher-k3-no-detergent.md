## The symptom pattern

The machine starts, builds pressure, and washes normally on plain water. Switch
the lance over to apply detergent and nothing comes through — no foam, no
smell of it, just water. The bottle's full and the machine otherwise seems
completely healthy.

## What's actually happening

Kärcher's own manual for the K3 states plainly that detergent can only be
added when the device is run in low-pressure mode — the high-pressure setting
physically won't draw it, by design, regardless of whether anything else is
wrong. That accounts for a good share of "no detergent" reports on its own:
the lance is simply left on the wrong setting.

Past that, detergent reaches the machine through a thin suction hose and a
small filter that sit down in the bottle. The manual's maintenance section
calls this filter out specifically as something to remove and clean, and
flags the hose itself as something to check for kinks — it's a narrow tube
carrying a small volume of liquid, and either one blocking or folding is
enough to stop suction completely while the rest of the machine works fine.

## Working through it

Start with the setting, not the hardware: confirm the lance is turned to Mix
(or your model's equivalent low-pressure detergent position) before you
suspect a blockage at all. If that's already correct, isolate the machine and
pull the suction hose to check it hasn't kinked in storage — a common cause
given how thin and flexible it is.

With the hose clear, turn to the filter on its end. It sits submerged in the
bottle and picks up whatever settles in the detergent over time; pulling it
off and rinsing it under a tap is usually all it takes. While you're there,
check the bottle isn't running low enough that the pickup end has lifted
clear of the liquid — an easy thing to miss once a bottle's mostly used.

## Verdict

Detergent infeed faults on the K3 are overwhelmingly a cleaning job, not a
repair: a rinsed filter and an unkinked hose fix the great majority of these
for nothing. Only a genuinely split or perished suction hose calls for a
replacement part, and that's a cheap one — there's no reason to think the
pump or the machine itself is at fault here.
