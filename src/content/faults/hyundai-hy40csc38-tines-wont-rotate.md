## The symptom pattern

The scarifier switches on and the motor clearly runs, but the tine shaft
underneath isn't turning the way it should — either it never starts
rotating, or it turns briefly and then stops. The lawn comes out
untouched even though the machine sounds like it's doing something.
Hyundai's troubleshooting table for this model treats "tines will not
rotate" as its own fault, with two named causes.

## What's actually happening

The first cause is the more mundane one: dirt and debris packed into the
gaps between the tines, physically jamming them from turning. The second
is distinct from that — vines, grass or other debris wrapped directly
around the tine shaft itself, which can stop rotation even if the tines
between them look reasonably clear. Both are named separately in the
table because they need slightly different fixes: clearing debris from
around the tines versus cleaning the shaft they're mounted on.

Neither cause involves anything electrical or mechanical failing — this
is a blockage fault, not a motor or drive problem, and the manual doesn't
suggest otherwise.

## Working through it

Release the bail first so the tines aren't being driven, then tip the
machine to get a clear look underneath. Before your hands go anywhere
near the tine shaft, open the battery hatch, remove the safety key, then
take the battery packs out — the manual states plainly that the safety
key comes out before batteries are installed or removed, and it's the
same isolation this job needs before you clear anything by hand.

Check the tines first for compacted dirt or debris sitting in the gaps
between them, and clear it out. If they still don't spin freely by hand
once that's done, look specifically for grass, vines or other debris
wound around the shaft itself rather than just caught between the tines —
that needs cleaning off the shaft directly, not just picking debris out
from around the tines.

Refit the battery packs first, then reinstall the safety key last — the
manual's own installation sequence does it in that order — and test on a
small clear patch of lawn before going back over the area you were
working on.

## Verdict

This is a cleaning job, not a repair — both causes the manual lists cost
nothing and need no parts. The only real discipline here is doing the
battery-and-safety-key removal first, in that order, before working
anywhere near the tines, exactly as the manual sets out for handling the
batteries in general.
