## The symptom pattern

The battery's in, the grass basket's on, and squeezing the switching bar on
the handle does nothing at all. On a mower, that reads like a dead motor or
a dead battery — but Einhell's own troubleshooting table for the GE-CM 36 Li
lists six possible causes for exactly this fault, and only two of them are
an actual electrical fault. The other four are ordinary conditions the
mower won't start under by design.

## What Einhell's own table says

Section 12 of the manual gives these causes for "Motor does not start", in
the order Einhell lists them: a defective capacitor, motor terminals or the
capacitor disconnected, the mower standing in high grass, a clogged mower
housing, the safety switch not inserted, and the battery not correctly
inserted. Four of those six are things anyone can check and fix without
tools: cut shorter grass first or raise the cutting height, clear a clogged
deck, press the handlebar safety switch before the switching bar, and
reseat the battery properly.

The safety switch point catches people out because it isn't obvious: the
manual states the mower "is equipped with a safety switch which must be
pressed before the switching bar can be activated" — the switching bar on
its own does nothing, by design, until that separate safety switch has been
pressed first. The battery cause is just as mechanical: the two packs need
to fully seat and the cover needs to latch, and Einhell is explicit that the
two batteries should be charged to the same level together, since the
mower's run time is set by whichever one is lower.

The remaining two causes — a defective capacitor, or motor terminals or the
capacitor disconnected — are both given the same remedy in the manual: "by
customer service center". There's no test procedure or user-level fix
offered for either, which is Einhell's own way of saying this is wiring
inside the motor housing, not something to open up and probe.

## Working through it

Check the easy, free causes first and in the order the manual gives them:
grass length and any clogging under the deck, then the safety-switch-before-
switching-bar sequence, then both batteries removed and refitted with the
cover properly latched. If the mower still won't start after all of that,
and both batteries are confirmed charged, the fault has narrowed down to the
capacitor or motor wiring — at that point Einhell's own documentation sends
it to a service centre rather than giving a repair step, so that's the right
next move rather than opening the motor housing on a guess.

## Verdict

This is one of the more forgiving "won't start" faults to diagnose, because
Einhell's own manual gives a short, complete list and four of the six
entries on it are free and immediate. Work through grass length, housing
clogging, the safety switch sequence and the battery seating before
assuming anything has actually failed. If it genuinely comes down to the
capacitor or motor wiring, that's a service job by the manufacturer's own
instructions, not a DIY repair with a published procedure to follow.
