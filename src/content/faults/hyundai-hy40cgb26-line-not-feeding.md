## The symptom pattern

The trimmer starts and runs fine, but the cutting line either won't come out
when the head is bumped on the ground, or it does feed and still leaves the
job looking patchy — grass missed in streaks rather than cut clean. Both
versions of the complaint trace back to the same small cutting head, and
Hyundai's own manual for this machine gives two specific causes for it.

## What's actually happening

The HY40CGB26's cutting head holds the line on a spool behind a cover held
by a release latch. Every pass through long or wet grass pushes trimmings up
into that housing, and enough of it packs around the spool to stop the line
paying out at all — the more common of the two causes the manual lists.

The other is simpler to overlook: Hyundai specifies 1.3mm, 1.4mm or 1.5mm
nylon line for this head. Fit anything thicker — easy to do, since bulk
trimmer line is sold loose by the reel with no machine written on it — and
it won't pass cleanly through the eyelets in the spool set, binding the feed
even with a perfectly clean head.

## Working through it

Pull the battery first; the manual is explicit that this job is done with it
disconnected. Release the cover latch and lift the cover off. Before
touching anything else, look at what's actually packed around the spool —
if it's full of compacted grass, that's very likely the whole fault, and
clearing it by hand or with a soft brush is free.

There's a small spring inside the head that holds the spool set together,
and it's loose rather than captive, so lift the spool out rather than
upending the head over a bin. With the head clear, check the line itself
against the 1.3–1.5mm recommendation before refitting anything — if it's
thicker, that's the second cause confirmed, and the fix is rewinding with
the right line rather than anything mechanical.

To rewind, fold a new length of line in half, hook the folded middle onto
the notch in the line spool, and wind each side into its own groove in the
same direction. Hook both ends into the notches on the spool set, fit the
spring into the outer spool, thread the ends through its eyelets, and seat
the spool back inside before pulling the ends firmly to release the line
from the holding inserts. Refit the cover and test: push the bump button
with a thumb while pulling the line with the other hand — it should pay out
smoothly. If it still doesn't, something in the rewind isn't seated, not a
new fault.

If the head is clean, the line is correctly sized, and it's still not
feeding or cutting cleanly, stop here — the manual's other listed causes for
this machine point to the battery or internal wiring rather than the
cutting head, and the wiring case routes straight to a service dealer
rather than giving a home fix.

## Verdict

A five-minute clean-out or a line swap covers everything this fault sheet
actually attributes to user error, and both are free or close to it. Open
the head and look before buying anything — a clog costs nothing to clear
and is the likelier of the two.
