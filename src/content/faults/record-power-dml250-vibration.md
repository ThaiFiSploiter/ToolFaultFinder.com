## The symptom pattern

The DML250 is turning, but it isn't turning smoothly — there's a vibration
or shake running through the machine that wasn't there before, whether
that shows up as a buzz through the bed, a wobble you can see in the
spinning workpiece, or just a lathe that feels less planted than it used
to.

## What's actually happening

Record Power's own troubleshooting table for this lathe treats vibration
as a symptom with five distinct, named causes, not a single fault to
chase down. In the order the manual itself gives them: the workpiece
being out of balance, worn spindle bearings, a worn drive belt, loose
motor mount bolts or handles, and the lathe standing on an uneven surface.
Two of those five have nothing to do with the machine's mechanical
condition at all.

## Working through it

Start with the workpiece before touching the lathe itself — an
out-of-balance blank is what Record Power list first, and it's also the
cheapest and quickest thing to rule out. Rough it closer to round, or
remount it, and see if the vibration changes.

If the workpiece isn't the cause, isolate the lathe at the socket and
check the two things that actually cost money to fix: grip the spindle
nose and feel for play (worn bearings), and look at the drive belt for
cracking, glazing or visible wear. The manual lists these as separate
causes, so check both rather than stopping at the first one you find.

Then check the two things that are neither expensive nor the workpiece's
fault: the motor mount bolts and the machine's handles, which vibration
itself can work loose over time, and whether the lathe is actually
standing level. A stand or bench that isn't flat will make even a
perfectly sound machine vibrate.

## The fix

Three of the five named causes cost nothing to fix: rebalancing or
remounting the workpiece, tightening mounting bolts and handles, and
levelling the machine. The other two — a worn belt or worn spindle
bearings — need a part, but both are inexpensive next to the lathe
itself, and Record Power's own list puts them behind the free checks, not
ahead of them.

## Verdict

Work through the causes in the order Record Power gives them, not in
whatever order seems most likely. The two setup checks — the workpiece
and the level of the machine — catch a real share of vibration complaints
on lathes like this at no cost at all, and the two hardware checks after
them cost a spanner and five minutes. A worn belt or worn bearings are
genuine possibilities on a lathe that's had real use, but they're the
manufacturer's fourth and second listed causes, not the first place to
look.
