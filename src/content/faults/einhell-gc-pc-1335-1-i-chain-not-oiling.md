## The symptom pattern

The saw starts and cuts normally, but the guide bar and chain run dry and
get hot during use rather than staying visibly wet with oil. On a
chainsaw that's a genuine wear risk, not just a performance niggle —
Einhell's own troubleshooting table for the GC-PC 1335/1 I lists exactly
this as "insufficient chain lubrication (the cutter rail and chain get
hot)," with only two possible causes.

## What Einhell's own table says

Section 9 of the manual gives two causes for insufficient chain
lubrication: an empty oil tank for the chain, or the oil lubrication
entry point on the guide rail having moved or blocked. The remedies are
just as short — top up the oil tank, or clean the oil entry hole and
groove on the guide rail.

The saw carries two separate reservoirs: a fuel tank for the petrol/oil
engine mixture, and a dedicated oil tank that feeds the bar and chain.
Einhell's manual is explicit that "whenever you refill the fuel tank with
petrol you must also top up the level of chain oil in the chain oil
tank," and names the oil type directly — "Chain, Bar and Sprocket Oil" —
with a plain warning: "never starve the bar and chain of lubricating oil."

The automatic oiler itself is a toothed-wheel-drive system that speeds up
as the engine is accelerated, feeding oil through the bar plate. Its flow
rate is set by an adjustment screw on the underside of the saw, but
Einhell's own documentation calls that setting factory-perfect and routes
any further adjustment to "your nearest authorized after-sales service
outlet" rather than giving an owner a setting procedure — the same
treatment the manual gives the carburettor.

## Working through it

With the engine switched off, check the oil tank level first — it's easy
to top up fuel and forget the second cap. If the tank is full, clean the
oil entry hole and the groove the chain runs in on the guide rail with a
proper cleaning tool; a blocked or displaced entry point is the manual's
other named cause. Einhell gives a direct way to confirm the fix worked
without guesswork: hold the running saw with the chain over a sheet of
paper at full speed for a few seconds, and the oil spray pattern on the
paper shows whether the oiler is delivering. If the tank is full, the
groove is clear, and the paper test still shows no oil, that's the point
the manual hands to a service outlet rather than the flow-adjustment
screw.

## Verdict

Both causes Einhell names are free, five-minute checks with the saw
switched off, and the paper test gives a clear pass/fail rather than a
guess. Don't reach for the underside adjustment screw before checking
the tank and the groove — and if both of those check out clean, Einhell's
own manual says the flow setting is a service-centre job, not an
owner-level adjustment. Running the saw dry in the meantime isn't worth
risking: a starved bar and chain run hot and wear faster with every cut.
