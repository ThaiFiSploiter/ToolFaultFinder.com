## The symptom pattern

The PowerMax Duo won't start, or cuts out while you're mowing, and the
error LED on the top panel is lit a steady red — not flashing, not off,
solid red. Gardena's manual treats the three LED states (solid red,
flashing red, off) as three separate rows in its troubleshooting table,
each with its own causes. This entry covers the **solid red** state only
— flashing red and no-light are different fault paths with different
causes, and folding them together would misattribute a cause that
belongs to a different LED state.

## What's actually happening

For the solid-red state specifically, the manual lists four possible
causes, each independent of the others, and they sit on a spectrum from
"costs nothing to check" to "needs a service visit":

- The battery's temperature is outside the permissible 0°C to +45°C
  charging/operating range.
- There are drops of water or moisture sitting between the battery
  contacts.
- Something is physically obstructing the blade or motor underneath the
  deck — and the manual's own fix for this one also has you check the
  blade is still at its 90° position afterwards, since dislodging an
  obstruction can knock it out of alignment.
- The cutting height is set too low. This is its own, separate cause in
  the table — it isn't tied to blade alignment, even though both involve
  the blade.

None of these four are electrical faults in the sense of needing a part
replaced. They're all either a wait, a wipe, a clear, or an adjustment.

## Working through it

Pull the battery before you do anything else — you don't want the blade
able to spin while you're checking underneath the deck.

Start with temperature, since it needs nothing but time: if the mower's
just come in from a cold shed or straight off a charge, give the battery
time to settle back into Gardena's stated 0°C–45°C range before trying
again.

With the battery still out, dry the contacts — both the battery's and
the mower's — with a dry cloth. The manual names this specifically:
moisture between the contacts is enough to trigger this exact LED state
on its own.

Tip the mower up and look underneath. Compacted grass, a stick or a
stone jammed against the blade will stop the motor and light this LED.
Clear it by hand with the battery still out, then check the blade still
sits at its proper 90° position using the manual's own alignment
procedure — the manual pairs this check with clearing the obstruction,
since dislodging something can tip the blade out of alignment.

Separately, check the cutting height. The manual lists this as its own
cause, not connected to blade alignment, so check it on its own merits
rather than assuming it's the same issue.

Only refit the battery and try again once you've been through all four.
If the LED is still solid red with nothing obstructing the blade, no
moisture on the contacts, and the blade properly aligned, Gardena's own
table stops offering causes at that point and calls it a fault in the
mower itself, routed to GARDENA Service.

## Verdict

This is a free-checks-first fault by the manual's own design — every
named cause behind the solid-red LED costs nothing and needs no part.
Work through temperature, moisture, obstruction (with the blade-alignment
check that comes with it) and cutting height in that order before
assuming anything's actually broken. If all four check out clean and the
LED stays lit, the manual itself has no further user-level step; it goes
straight to GARDENA Service, not a part you can buy and fit yourself.
