## The symptom pattern

The spindle comes off the lathe looking almost threaded — a faint spiral
ridge running along the length of the work instead of a clean, smooth
surface. It's a common enough problem that owners often assume the
worst: worn headstock bearings letting the spindle wander as it turns.
Record Power's own support answer to this exact question says otherwise.

## What's actually happening

Asked directly whether spiralling might be bearing wear, Record Power's
knowledge base answer doesn't confirm it — it redirects to something much
simpler: "the most likely cause of spiraling is that your centres are out
of alignment." On a lathe like the DML305, held between a headstock centre
and a tailstock centre, even a small mismatch between the two changes the
angle the tool cuts at as it travels along the work, which is exactly the
kind of error that shows up as a regular spiral rather than random
roughness.

The same answer names two further causes, both echoed in the machine's own
troubleshooting guide under a related symptom: blunt turning tools, and
incorrect lathe speed. The troubleshooting table lists a blunt tool as the
cause of a machine that "bogs down during cutting," with the fix simply
"sharpen turning tools" — a dull edge doesn't cut cleanly, it tears and
skips, which reads as a spiral pattern rather than a torn one at a glance.
Speed matters for the same reason: too fast or too slow for the diameter
being turned changes how the tool engages the wood on each pass.

## Working through it

Start with alignment, since Record Power name it first. With the lathe
switched off and unplugged, check that the headstock and tailstock centres
line up — bring the tailstock up to (but not touching) the headstock
centre and look for any offset, vertically or horizontally. Realign the
tailstock along the bed if there's a mismatch.

Next, check the edge on your turning tools. A gouge or skew that's lost its
edge doesn't announce itself as obviously as a blunt drill bit does, but it
tears wood fibres rather than shearing them, and that shows as texture in
the finish. Sharpen before assuming anything else is wrong.

Then check your speed against the six fixed settings this lathe offers —
350, 670, 1025, 1500, 2225 and 3250 rpm — and make sure you're not running
noticeably too fast or too slow for the diameter of the piece on the
lathe. If the cut also feels heavy or is bogging the motor down, ease off
the depth of cut at the same time; it's a separate line in Record Power's
own guide but it can show up alongside a poor finish.

## Verdict

This is a setup problem, not a parts problem, in the overwhelming majority
of cases, and it costs nothing to fix: realign the centres, sharpen your
tools, and check the speed is right for the work. Record Power's own
answer to the bearing-wear question people usually ask is that it almost
certainly isn't that — so don't reach for the headstock until alignment,
sharpness and speed have all been ruled out properly. If the spiral is
still there after all three, that's a legitimate case to put to Record
Power's own support rather than guess further.
